## Patterns to keep

- The claim first, then the mechanism that makes it true ("an error fails the run, so ...").
- "X, not Y" contrasts to draw a precise line. A colon introduces the reason. No em-dash chains.
- Counts carry their denominator or their test ("98%, 77 questions × 3 runs").
- Caveats sit beside the claim and are specific ("on synthetic data").
- Plain operational verbs: reads, checks, fails, reconciles, writes. First person singular; no "we" for solo work.
- Roles, not names, for people and clients. Employer and client work is described, never named or quantified with real figures.

## Not his voice

Hype adjectives, exclamation marks, question headings used as hooks, "we're passionate about",
service lists that would fit any consultancy, numbers with no method.
